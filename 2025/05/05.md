## 🧠 English

### ✅ What I did
- Reviewed conjunctions
- Studied the subjunctive mood and auxiliary verbs

### 💡 What I noticed / learned
- In the subjunctive, the marker is the past tense of the auxiliary; when you spot it, suspect the subjunctive  
- Phrases like “it’s time…” and “I wish”, and the future-subjunctive “should” have no past‑tense auxiliary  
- There are present, past, and future subjunctive forms  
- Often the “if” clause is omitted and replaced with structures like “without” or omitted entirely  
- Inversion frequently occurs in subjunctive constructions  
- “As if” can also trigger a subjunctive  
- “Will” expresses 100% certainty, whereas “may” conveys about 50% probability  
- “Will you” can be used in three different ways  
- “May I” is a polite request; “you may” shouldn’t be used toward superiors  
- “Must” vs. “have to”: the former is subjective obligation, the latter objective necessity  
- Negative forms of modals shift their meanings significantly
